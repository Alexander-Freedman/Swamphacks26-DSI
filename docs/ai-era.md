# Hackathons when everyone builds with AI: what wins, where the time goes, what still breaks

Research notes, Oct 16 2026. These are general findings, not tied to a specific idea.
Tags: **[primary]** means I read the source page itself. **[secondary]** means a write-up of the source. **[snippet]** means I only saw a search-result summary and didn't open the page. **[vendor]** means the source sells something related. **[est]** means it's my own estimate.

## TL;DR for a 2-person, 36h, expo-judged, 3-min-demo team

1. **A working demo is the floor now, not a differentiator.** A judge with 15 years and 12+ events behind her says every team at two 2026 hackathons had working code, a polished UI and real data, with no slide-only entries and no crashes. What separated the winners was *product sense*: they could say the problem, why existing fixes fall short, and what theirs does, in one sentence ([Semgrep, May 2026](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect/) [primary]).
2. **What judges mark down in AI-era entries:** generic AI-looking UI (purple gradients, Inter font, three icon cards), piling on 8-10 features that don't connect ("zero product sense"), a chatbot where a form would do, an AI voiceover, and **a team that can't explain its own code**. Sources: [Daren Tan, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daren-tan_hackathonkakitips-darentechtalks-activity-7362996665345069057-kZ5H), [HackerNoon judge, Jan 2026](https://hackernoon.com/from-the-judges-seat-standing-out-in-hackathons-in-the-age-of-ai), [Chainguard Vibelympics](https://www.chainguard.dev/unchained/the-only-rule-dont-look-at-the-code), [lost-hackathon postmortem, Mar 2026](https://shrit.substack.com/p/lost-so-bad-got-humble-again) (all [primary]). SwampHacks XII's first criterion is "Technical Understanding", and the event has a Least Vibe Coded award (per our strategy.md), so the last point counts double for us.
3. **Where the freed time should go:** testing with real people and iterating, one deep "hard part" over breadth, real or live data, a custom look, and rehearsal. The best first-hand evidence: the ElevenLabs SF 1st-place team tested their voice agent on real call recipients and changed the voice after people hung up on it. A team there said Lovable "saved us 20 hours", and that time went to user testing, iteration and fine-tuning ([PostHog, Mar 2025](https://posthog.com/blog/elevenlabs-hackathon) [primary]).
4. **AI is fast at greenfield scaffolding and slow at the last 30%:** integration, env/deploy, auth callbacks, version-specific APIs, hardware, and debugging code nobody on the team read. Controlled studies range from **+56% faster** (a greenfield HTTP server, 2023) to **19% slower** (experienced devs on large repos they knew well, 2025).
5. **Plan around the failure modes:** deploy a hello-world by hour 2-3, use managed auth or no auth, pin library versions, put a cache or fallback on the demo path for every LLM/API call, and record the backup video Saturday night.

## Q1. Once AI lets most teams ship fast, what do judges reward?

### Evidence that the baseline moved up
- **Semgrep "The AI Hackathon Effect" (May 2026)** [primary]. The author has organized or judged 12+ hackathons over 15 years. Before AI, a few teams had both a strong idea and working code, others ran out of time or brought decks with wireframes, and judges "graded on a generous curve". At the two 2026 events (Semgrep, and DataGrail, a 24h event), every project worked, with "no slide-only decks, no wireframe-only entries, no major demo crashes", plus polished UI and real data. Judges struggled to pick. The winners had "a clear point of view" and could state the problem, why existing solutions fall short, and their solution in one sentence. That clarity showed in their scope and in what they cut. Her verdict: "the best projects built the right thing, not the most." https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect/
- **An AI lab's "Built with" model hackathon (2026) winners** [primary]. Four of the five winners weren't professional developers. They were a personal-injury lawyer, a cardiologist, a Ugandan ministry staffer and a musician. All five targeted real problems they knew firsthand (housing permits, post-appointment care, road investment), and some had real users or deployments (a city was considering one; another is deployed in three countries). Separately reported: 13,000 entrants [secondary, snippet]. **Domain insight and a real problem beat raw engineering.** https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon
- **Devfolio "Push to Prod" with an AI lab (Aug 2026, Bengaluru)** [primary]. 81 submissions, 43 teams, 5-hour build. The strongest teams "chose one ambitious bet and made it work well instead of partially building many features". AI cut scaffolding time, so teams spent it "on architecture, product decisions and the uncertain core of their idea". https://devfolio.co/blog/pushed-to-prod/

### What judges say they mark down now
- **Design sameness.** "Projects look alike now because of vibe coding." Teams pack in 8-10 features that don't connect ("Zero product sense"). LLMs get used where a deterministic method would do (in healthcare a judge asks why you chose non-determinism), and chatbots replace a simple form ([Daren Tan, judge, LinkedIn 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/daren-tan_hackathonkakitips-darentechtalks-activity-7362996665345069057-kZ5H) [primary]).
- **AI-feeling ideas and UI, over-building, AI voiceovers** ([Abgar Simonean, HackerNoon, Jan 5 2026](https://hackernoon.com/from-the-judges-seat-standing-out-in-hackathons-in-the-age-of-ai) [primary]). He estimates about 90% of projects show AI influence. Judges can sense brainstorms that came out of AI. Teams that add features faster than they can explain them lose credibility. One-prompt Lovable sites "feel AI-made". Presenting in your own voice and on camera beats AI narration.
- **Polish without depth, and claims without proof.** Projects that win show a before/after story, a README, an architecture diagram, known limitations, and **honest AI disclosure** ("AI as assistant for boilerplate/debugging"). "AI built everything" claims did not stand out ([Amit Kumar Singh, DEV](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06) [primary]).
- **Can't explain the code means you lose.** At a 24h event (Mar 2026), the sole coder on a team "coded almost everything with a coding assistant", couldn't answer the judges' backend questions, and lost. His lesson: "never rely blindly on AI code" ([shrit substack](https://shrit.substack.com/p/lost-so-bad-got-humble-again) [primary]).
- **Judges notice when the UI was barely prompted.** At Chainguard's Vibelympics (Dec 2025), a judge called out "a purple UI shade" as a sign the designer had barely prompted the interface. When another judge tried a project live during judging, it hit a timeout. The winner had built a security tool using adversarial agents to review each other's PRs, and judges weighted security, vibes and functionality equally ([Chainguard](https://www.chainguard.dev/unchained/the-only-rule-dont-look-at-the-code) [primary]). Why AI UIs default to purple: Tailwind UI's `bg-indigo-500` default spread through training data ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website) [snippet]).
- **"Generic wrapper around OpenAI no longer impressive"** ([lablab.ai](https://lablab.ai/blog/ai-to-code-winning-hackathons-guide) [snippet, vendor]). "Most hackathon ideas look the same: a chatbot, a nice UI, and a demo that works on the data the team typed in that morning." What wins is "the demo where something real happens", using live, verifiable data ([TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/how-to-win-a-hackathon-with-ai) [primary, vendor]).

### Judging is getting more evidence-based (repo vs claims)
- **Devfolio built an agent** that walks the repo and writes a cited report on whether the code backs up the pitch's claims. It flags mismatches (a "custom model" that is really a wrapper around a pretrained one), checks whether the hard part is actually built, and looks for trivial or generated filler. Humans make the final call. Their point: a polished README becomes "a longer list of claims to check" ([Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/the-discerning-machine/) [primary]).
- **MLH rules template:** "You may use LLM/ChatGPT/AI, but must state how you did so in your Devpost submission." "You must credit any tools used and be clear on what you made and what was generated by having a detailed readme." Undisclosed use means disqualification and a report to cheating@mlh.io. "Your project should not be a reskin of an existing AI tool." No reused code, and no work before the event ([MLH guide](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/rules-for-your-hackathon) [primary]). Some events go further: "AI-generated code must not constitute the majority" ([TISB](https://tisbhacks.tisb.org/ai-policy.html) [snippet]).
- **Anti-vibe events and awards exist:** a "$5000 De-Vibed Hackathon" ([luma](https://luma.com/dj3k3tri) [snippet]). SwampHacks XI gave a "Least Vibecoded" prize (Pocket Money, per our strategy.md appendix); I couldn't find it on the open web.

### Ranking: what matters more vs less now (my synthesis of the above)
| Factor | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Problem clarity / product sense / one-sentence story | **Up a lot** | Semgrep, Push to Prod, an AI lab winners |
| Team can explain and defend the code | **Up a lot** | SwampHacks XII "Technical Understanding" criterion, lost-team postmortem, Devfolio claim auditing |
| Depth on one hard part (not feature count) | **Up** | Push to Prod, Daren Tan, Semgrep |
| Real data / real users / something real happens live | **Up** | TinyFish, an AI lab winners, ElevenLabs winner |
| Distinctive design (not default AI look) | **Up** | Tan, Simonean, Chainguard |
| Prize/sponsor fit | Same (still strong; see strategy.md Part 4) | no AI-era change found |
| "It works at all" / basic polish | **Down as a differentiator** (still required) | Semgrep: everyone had it |
| Live-demo reliability | Same or up | the bar is "no crash", and a single timeout is noticed (Chainguard) |
| Honest AI disclosure | **Required** | MLH, SwampHacks rules |

## Q2. How do AI-heavy winning teams spend the freed time?

First-hand accounts with timelines are rare. Here's what exists:

- **User testing and iteration (best evidence).** ElevenLabs hackathon SF, 2 days (PostHog, Mar 2025) [primary]. The 1st-place team (DealWise) tested their voice agent on real call recipients. People hung up on a clear, professional voice, so the team added pauses, a mild accent and hesitation, and people then stayed on to the end. The 2nd-place idea came from a teammate's own incident. One team said Lovable "saved us 20 hours of coding", and that time went to "user testing, iteration and model fine-tuning". https://posthog.com/blog/elevenlabs-hackathon
- **One-shot build, then polish.** PromptLayer's Vibe Coding Olympics: the round-3 winner "wrote a precise initial prompt, landed a fast one-shot build, and spent the remaining time polishing", while a rival who tried to parallelize agents lost ([PromptLayer](https://blog.promptlayer.com/we-hosted-the-first-vibe-coding-olympics/) [primary]; rounds were under 15 min, so the timeline doesn't transfer).
- **Cursor Hackathon HCMC (Nov 2025), 4-hour event, 1st place** [primary]. He spent about 1h designing the idea before arriving, choosing between 2 ideas. In hour 1, plan mode plus agent mode produced a first version that was "only like 5% done" and "looked more like a website than a game". Then about 3h of bug fixing across 2-5 parallel Cursor chats, with git commits as checkpoints. He cut a feature, swapped 3D for 2D when 3D broke, and **hard-coded a demo step** for reliability. His conclusion: "the most important part was the 2-minute demo"; "done is more important than perfect". 700+ participants, 65+ submissions. https://nvbinh.substack.com/p/cursor-hackathon-a-reflection
- **Several quick prototypes.** At a Deloitte/AWS healthcare hackathon, teams "designed and built three prototypes within 48 hours" using spec-driven AI tooling ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/nz/en/Industries/life-sciences-health-care/perspectives/code-for-care.html) [snippet]). A JetBrains 48h AI hackathon produced 41 prototypes [snippet]. No source shows that "build 2-3 and pick" wins more often. My read [est]: for a 2-person 36h event, a 1-2h spike on the riskiest piece of each finalist idea, then commit, fits our "lock the idea before hacking" plan better than building parallel full prototypes.
- **Depth over breadth.** Push to Prod: "five focused hours to prove the hardest part of an ambitious bet". The top teams made one bet work ([Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/pushed-to-prod/) [primary]).
- **Using AI for the plumbing and keeping the core human.** "The teams that win still hand-write the 20% of logic that makes their project different" ([Masai](https://www.masaischool.com/blog/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon-in-2026-full-guide/) [snippet]). Simonean: let AI scaffold, then write the key parts yourself ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/from-the-judges-seat-standing-out-in-hackathons-in-the-age-of-ai) [primary]).
- **Time split guidance.** "60% building the core feature, 20% polishing UI and bug fixes, 20% presentation" ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/how-to-use-ai-tools-win-hackathons-2026/) [snippet, vendor-ish]). TinyFish's 90-min plan gives about 30% of the time to demo-readiness plus rehearsal ([TinyFish](https://www.tinyfish.ai/blog/how-to-win-a-hackathon-with-ai) [primary, vendor]).
- **Backup video and demo reliability.** Guides agree on recording a backup. One first-person account chose a recorded video because a dependency server was unreliable, while other teams sat through "long seconds of awkward silence" when their live apps froze ([networked substack](https://networked.substack.com/p/the-perils-of-the-live-demo) [snippet]).
- **Prep beforehand is the hidden edge.** The AI lab x Forum Ventures NYC winner (Sep 2025, about 8h build, $15k credits) had a a coding assistant setup he'd refined over months, which split work across specialized agents while other teams were still setting up ([36kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3823966768943239) [snippet, secondary]). That's allowed under MLH rules as long as no *project* code is written in advance; tooling, agent configs and prompts are fair game.

**What this suggests for us [est]:** take the hours AI saves and spend them, in order, on (a) a 10-minute hallway test with 3-5 real attendees by Saturday midday, (b) one depth feature that a judge can poke at (eval numbers, a real dataset, a live sensor), (c) a custom visual identity, (d) a "why did we do X" drill so both of us can defend every choice, and (e) recording the backup video and rehearsing the pitch 5+ times.

## Q3. Where are AI coding assistants slow or unreliable?

### Headline productivity evidence
| Study | Setting | Result |
|---|---|---|
| METR RCT (Jul 2025) [primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089), [blog](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) | 16 experienced OSS devs, 246 tasks in their own mature repos (~5 yrs experience on them), Cursor + a coding assistant 3.5/3.7 | **19% slower** with AI. They predicted a 24% speedup and still believed afterward that they were 20% faster. Experts predicted 38-39% faster |
| METR follow-up (Feb 24 2026) [secondary](https://blog.robbowley.net/2026/04/04/metrs-developer-productivity-research-2026-update/) → https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ | 57 devs, 800+ tasks | Data called unreliable: devs refused to work without AI, and 30-50% avoided submitting tasks they didn't want to do without AI. Point estimates were reportedly -18% (CI -38%..+9%) for returning devs and -4% for new devs [snippet]. Sources disagree on the sign, so check before quoting. METR thinks speedup is "probably" higher now |
| Google RCT (2024, ICSE-SEIP 2025) [arXiv 2410.12944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944) [secondary] | 96 Google engineers, enterprise task | about **21% faster** (96 vs 114 min), wide CI |
| Copilot RCT (Peng et al. 2023) [arXiv 2302.06590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590) [secondary] | 95 freelancers, greenfield JS HTTP server | **55.8% faster** |
| Uplevel (2024) [PDF](https://static.simonwillison.net/static/2025/uplevel-genai-productivity.pdf), [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/3540579/devs-gaining-little-if-anything-from-ai-coding-assistants.html) [secondary, vendor, observational] | ~800 devs | **41% more bugs** in PRs, cycle time -1.7 min ("inconsequential") |
| Faros AI telemetry (2025) [secondary](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/ai-productivity-paradox-engineering-delivery) [vendor] | 10k+ devs, ~1.2k teams | high-AI teams: +21% tasks, +98% PRs merged, but **PR review time +91%**, PR size +154%, bugs/dev +9%; flat at company level |
| DORA 2025 [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025), [Splunk](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/state-of-devops) [secondary] | ~5,000 pros | 90% adoption; AI now linked to **higher throughput but also higher instability** (more change failures and rework); median 2h/day with AI; about 30% have little or no trust in AI code |
| AI lab internal (Dec 2025) [primary](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic) | 132 engineers, 200k a coding assistant transcripts | self-reported +50% productivity; 59% of work uses a coding assistant; top uses are debugging 55%, code understanding 42%, features 37%; 27% of the work wouldn't have been done otherwise; >half can fully delegate only 0-20% of their work; design/planning gets the smallest gains |

**Takeaway:** a hackathon is greenfield, small, with no legacy and no reviewers, which is the setting where gains are largest (the +56% and +21% studies). The slow parts are at the edges: integration, environment, external services, unfamiliar or new APIs, and code nobody read.

### Developer surveys
- **Stack Overflow 2025** (49k respondents) [primary](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai): **66%** cite "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite", and **45.2%** say "debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming". 3.1% highly trust AI accuracy, 29.6% somewhat trust it, 45.7% distrust it. For complex tasks, only 4.4% say "very well", while 41.4% of professionals say bad or very poor. **75.8% don't plan to use AI for deployment and monitoring**. 31% use agents. Only ~15% say they "vibe code".
- **JetBrains State of Dev Ecosystem 2025** (24.5k) [snippet; primary page 403](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2025/10/state-of-developer-ecosystem-2025/): 85% use AI regularly, ~88-90% save at least 1h/week, ~20% save 8h+/week. Top concerns are code quality (23%) and limited understanding of complex code and logic (18%).
- **Addy Osmani's "70% problem"** [primary](https://addyosmani.com/agentic-engineering/the-70-percent-problem/): AI gets you about 70% fast, and **the last 30% takes longer than the first 70%**. That 30% is edge cases, race conditions, accessibility, hidden security flaws, integration, performance and deployment. Better specs raise AI's share to about 80-85%.

### By failure area (time costs where reported)
| Area | Evidence | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontend↔backend integration** | Agents "often produce frontend-only sites" and "mask the lack of real data flow with fancy visual effects". FullStack-Dev beat the previous best by +38.2 on backend tests vs +8.7 on frontend, so backend and data flow is the weak side ([FullStack-Agent, arXiv 2602.03798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03798) [primary abstract]). A human study of Replit/Bolt/Firebase Studio (288 apps) found "a persistent gap between visual polish and functional reliability" ([arXiv 2512.18080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18080) [primary abstract]) | none reported. [est] 1-4h per broken seam if the contract isn't pinned early |
| **Deploy and env config** | "Works locally, breaks on Vercel": the `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix, `.env.local` not deployed, values baked in at build time and needing a redeploy, auth URL settings ([blog collection](https://www.iloveblogs.blog/post/nextjs-vercel-env-variables-fix), [NextAuth prod debugging](https://johnkavanagh.co.uk/articles/nextauth-production-debugging-vercel/) [snippet]). 75.8% of devs won't use AI for deployment (SO 2025) | [est] 0.5-3h if you deploy late; ~15 min if a hello-world goes up in hour 1. Strategy.md's "deployed URL by hour 6" is right; earlier is better |
| **Auth / OAuth** | AI builders "often hardcode localhost or wrong redirect paths"; errors like redirect_uri_mismatch and invalid_client. AI-written auth skips the `state` param, stores tokens in localStorage, and accepts any redirect ([miracuves](https://miracuves.com/blog/oauth-redirect-uri-fix-cursor-builds/), [vibeappscanner](https://vibeappscanner.com/oauth-security-best-practices) [snippet, vendor]). Veracode: AI code had vulnerabilities in **45%** of 80 tasks; XSS went unblocked **86%** of the time ([Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/) [secondary]) | [est] managed auth (Auth0/Clerk/Supabase): 0.5-1.5h; hand-rolled OAuth: 2-5h with callback debugging. Skip auth unless a prize needs it |
| **Hallucinated or outdated APIs and packages** | USENIX Sec 2025: **19.7%** of samples had at least one hallucinated package (open models ~21.7%, commercial ~5.2%), and 43% of hallucinations repeat every run ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/spracklen), figures [secondary]). 2026 replication on frontier models: **4.6-6.1%** ([alphaxiv 2605.17062](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.17062.md) [snippet]). Deprecated-API usage of 25-38% across 7 LLMs; accuracy drops from 56.1% to 32.5% on APIs released after the training cutoff ([tianpan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-17-deprecated-api-trap-ai-coding-agents) [snippet, secondary to ICSE'25 / arXiv 2406.09834]) | [est] 15-60 min per incident. Worst on **new sponsor SDKs** (alpha SDKs like DeepSpace are high risk). Fix: paste current docs into context, pin versions |
| **Hardware / embedded** | EmbedAgent (2025): best model **55.6%** pass@1 with a schematic and 50% when it has to design the schematic; ESP-IDF migration only **29.4%**; Arduino→ESP32 21.4-27.8%; 41.9% of ESP32 code had syntax or header errors ([arXiv 2506.11003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11003) [primary abstract], [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11003)). PCEval: LLMs "struggle significantly" with breadboard layout and pin wiring ([arXiv 2601.02404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02404) [primary abstract]) | [est] roughly half of the generated firmware fails the first time; budget 3-8h for sensor bring-up. Use Arduino/MicroPython over ESP-IDF |
| **CSS / design taste** | the default purple/Inter/3-card look (see Q1). Agents guess Tailwind colors and add a brand color without its shade scale ([snippet](https://dev.to/bourhaouta/tailwind-color-palettes-the-ai-way-37b1)). an AI lab engineers keep "taste" and design work for humans ([an AI lab](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)) | [est] 2-4h of deliberate design work (pick a palette, a type pair, one reference site) to escape the default look |
| **API rate limits and keys** | Gemini free tier is about 10-15 RPM and 250-1,500 requests/day depending on the model; Pro models were reportedly dropped from free in Apr 2026. Limits are **per project, so extra keys don't add quota**. RPD only resets at midnight Pacific ([tinkerllm](https://tinkerllm.com/blog/gemini-api-free-tier-limits-rate-quotas/), [yingtu](https://yingtu.ai/en/blog/google-gemini-api-free-tier) [snippet, unverified]) | Risk at the expo: several judges in a row, or a dev loop, burn the daily quota. Cache demo responses and add backoff |
| **Flaky live demos** | Cursor winner hard-coded a demo step. A Chainguard finalist timed out live. Semgrep's 2026 events had no crashes, so a crash now stands out more | [est] 1h to build a "demo mode" (cached responses, seeded data, offline fallback) |
| **Debugging AI-written code / context loss** | 45% say debugging AI code takes longer (SO). GitClear: copy/paste lines exceeded moved lines for the first time in 2024; 5+ line duplicate blocks up 8x; refactoring fell from 25% to under 10% of changes ([DevClass](https://devclass.com/2025/02/20/ai-is-eroding-code-quality-states-new-in-depth-report/), [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/technical-direction/how-ai-generated-code-accelerates-technical-debt) [secondary]). SWE-Bench Pro (multi-file, hours-to-days tasks): best model **23.3%** at launch, Sep 2025 ([arXiv 2509.16941](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16941v2) [snippet]). METR time horizon: agents finish about 50-min human tasks at 50% reliability (a coding assistant 3.7, Mar 2025), doubling every ~7 months; reportedly ~2h40m by late 2025 ([arXiv 2503.14499](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14499v1), later figure [snippet]). Practical rule from a guide: if you've debugged the same component for over an hour, start fresh ([awesome-vibe-coding-guide](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/analyticalrohit/awesome-vibe-coding-guide) [snippet]) | Agents are reliable on chunks under about 1-2 human-hours; past that, 50/50. Keep tasks small, keep NOTES.md as shared memory, and commit often |

## Q4. Realistic time ranges with AI (2 people, 36h)

No source gives measured hackathon task times with AI. Everything below is **[est]**, anchored on the evidence above (greenfield speedups of 20-56%, the slow last 30%, agent reliability on tasks under ~1-2h). Ranges are wall-clock for one person driving an assistant. The low end assumes a familiar stack and a happy path; the high end assumes one integration surprise.

| Task | Range [est] | What blows it up | Anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scaffold (Next.js/Vite + API) + first deploy to Vercel/Render | **0.5-1.5h** | env vars, monorepo build config, Python backend hosting | env pitfalls above; 75.8% won't trust AI with deploy |
| Auth via managed provider (Auth0/Clerk/Supabase) | **0.5-1.5h** | callback/redirect URLs differing between localhost and prod | OAuth snippets above |
| Hand-rolled OAuth or custom auth | **2-5h** | redirect mismatch, cookies, security gaps | Veracode 45% |
| DB setup (Supabase/Postgres/Firebase + schema + seed) | **0.5-2h** | RLS/permissions, migrations, wiring the frontend to real data (agents fake it) | FullStack-Agent |
| Map UI (Leaflet/Mapbox, markers, a few layers) | **1-3h** basic, **+2-4h** for custom layers/heatmaps/time slider | tile keys, projection/GeoJSON format issues, performance with 1k+ points | none found; est |
| ML baseline + honest eval (sklearn/XGBoost or an LLM classifier on a labeled set) | **3-6h**, including **1-3h hand-labeling 50-150 examples** | data cleaning, leakage, making the eval numbers defensible to judges | AI/ML track wants "non-trivial... evals" (strategy.md) |
| LLM feature with structured output + caching/fallback | **1-3h** | rate limits (10-15 RPM free), JSON schema drift | Gemini limits |
